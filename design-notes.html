Signal-to-Fix Engine — Design Notes

How one platform serves three stages of AI adoption, shown through the support-to-product loop.

The Signal-to-Fix loop

Goal: shorten the time from the first affected conversation to every rep giving the right answer.

Show architecture ▾
One platform. Three tiers.

Every enterprise runs on the same Signal-to-Fix Engine and the same data model. Each picks the tooling that matches how it works today, and can move up a tier without re-integrating.

Why each tier is faster

TierWhat changes, and why it removes waitingEffect
Human-led
baseline
  • Signal Agent clusters, CX Lead reviews: conversations are scored and grouped automatically; the CX Lead reviews by exception (low-fit conversations are flagged) and reports. Confident at ~38 conversations.
  • Manual alert: the CX Lead writes and files a ticket; it waits in the product team's queue until someone reads and prioritizes it.
  • Dashboard: the engineer clicks through conversations in the support platform's dashboard, outside their own tools.
 ~100 conversations without the right guidance
Mixed
  • Webhook: the report lands in the product team's queue in one click, with summary and evidence attached, so there's no ticket to write. Helps most during working hours.
  • MCP: the engineer's AI assistant queries conversations in plain language, next to the engineer's own logs and code in the same session. Investigation drops from ~2h to ~30m.
 ~70
Agent-led
  • Release Agent: correlates clusters with what recently changed ("missing apartment deliveries" ↔ "last release changed shipping labels"). A known change plus matching complaints reaches confidence at ~10 conversations instead of ~38, hours earlier.
  • Engineering Agent: Picks up the report immediately and routes it to the owning engineer, hands over the support platform's evidence plus the correlated change, relays the engineer's info request, and pushes answers back the moment they're ready.
  • KB Agent: applies the enterprise's policies and language, publishes rep-facing guidance immediately, and routes customer-facing messages to the CX Lead.
  • Why agents, not just better tools: MCP only works while an engineer has a session open. An A2A task stays open between human steps, so nothing waits for someone to look, relay, or remember.

Quality signals

Quality of experiencequality of the customer's experience
Frustration at startHow upset the customer is when they arrive, before support has done anything.low · medium · high

Why it matters: separates what the customer brought in from what support did. Without it, reps get blamed for customers who arrived angry, and incident severity is understated: high entry frustration means customers already hit the problem, maybe more than once.

Used for: incident severity; normalizing the trajectory signal.

Sentiment trajectoryHow the customer's sentiment changed from the start of the conversation to the end.improved · flat · worsened

Why it matters: the part of the experience support actually controls. A customer who arrives furious and leaves calm is a good conversation; a raw sentiment score would call it bad.

Used for: measuring whether guidance is working; a worsened trajectory feeds the "ended poorly" outcome.

Resolved in conversationWhether the customer's problem was solved before the conversation ended.yes · no · escalated

Why it matters: the outcome that drives cost (repeat contacts, escalations) and CSAT. During an incident it's the clearest sign reps don't have the right answer.

Used for: the "ended poorly" outcome on the impact chart.

Triagewhere the conversation belongs and who can act on it
Likely root causeThe most probable reason the customer contacted support, with a confidence score.product issue · policy · knowledge gap · external

Why it matters: different causes need different owners. Only product issues go to the product team; knowledge gaps go to support training; external causes (theft, carrier) go nowhere. Routing everything everywhere is how teams learn to ignore alerts.

Used for: deciding whether a cluster is reported to the product team.

Cluster fitHow closely a conversation matches the rest of its cluster.0–1

Why it matters: lets the CX Lead review by exception instead of reading every conversation. Low-fit conversations (e.g., porch theft inside a "delivered but not received" cluster) are flagged as unsure. The CX Lead's removals and additions become labeled data for cluster precision and recall.

Used for: human review of the cluster before it's reported.

Deliberately not included · how I'd validate the signals
Not included
  • Empathy / agent tone scores — useful for rep coaching, not incident detection. Different loop, different buyer.
  • CSAT prediction — would duplicate the three experience signals without adding an action.
How I'd validate the signals
  • Agreement with human QA labels on a sample of ~200 conversations, per signal.
  • CX Lead removals and additions → precision and recall of clusters over time.
  • Resolved-in-conversation vs. actual repeat contacts within 7 days.

Core data model

ObjectKey attributes
Casecase_id, started_at, channel, signals: frustration_at_start, trajectory, resolved, likely_root_cause {type, confidence}; issues: [{issue_id, cluster_fit, added_by}]
Issueissue_id, title, summary, likely_root_cause {type, confidence}, case_count, first_seen, state
Evidenceissue_id, case_id, turn, quote, request_id (set when the quote answers an InfoRequest)
InfoRequestrequest_id, issue_id, question, answers_needed, answers {count, breakdown}, each answer linked to Evidence
Guidanceguidance_id, issue_id, audience (rep / customer), text, approval (auto / pending / approved), status (draft / live / retired)

Issue lifecycle: reported → investigating → needs_info → resolved

Relationships: a Case can belong to more than one Issue, so membership lives on the Case, with cluster_fit relative to that Issue and added_by (system / cx_lead) recording the CX Lead's review. Evidence always points to the Issue it supports, and to the InfoRequest when it's an answer.

Core API

Resource-shaped: one endpoint per object, for code and for the support platform's own dashboard. Every other interface is built on it.

EndpointPurpose
GET /v1/issues · GET /v1/issues/{id}List and read Issues
GET /v1/issues/{id}/casesCases in an Issue, with signals
GET /v1/cases/{id}/evidenceEvidence for a Case
POST /v1/issues/{id}/info-requests · GET /v1/info-requests/{id}Create and read InfoRequests
POST /v1/guidance · PATCH /v1/guidance/{id}Draft, approve, retire Guidance
PATCH /v1/issues/{id}Update Issue state

Webhook

Event-shaped: pushes an Issue into the enterprise's tracker the moment the CX Lead reports it. The payload carries enough to triage without logging into the support platform.

issue.reported

  "event": "issue.reported",
  "event_id": "evt_8f2c",
  "issue": { "issue_id": "ISS-1042", "title": "Delivered but not received",
             "likely_root_cause": { "type": "product_issue", "confidence": 0.91 },
             "case_count": 37, "first_seen": "Mon 12:48" },
  "evidence": [ { "issue_id": "ISS-1042", "case_id": "C-40811", "turn": 1,

  "investigate_via": { "mcp_server": "signal-mcp", "issue_id": "ISS-1042" }

The Issue lifecycle maps onto A2A task states

the support platform sends investigate_issue to the Engineering Agent. The task's state is the Issue's state:

A2A task stateIssue stateWhat's happening
submittedreportedThe support platform hands over the Issue with evidence and correlated changes
workinginvestigatingEngineering Agent routes to the owning engineer
input-requiredneeds_infoEngineering Agent needs customer answers; The support platform asks customers and replies on the task
completedresolvedFix verified; resolution returned as a task artifact

    "message": "Can't reproduce. Ask affected customers: 'Do you live in an apartment or
                building with a mailroom or parcel locker?' Need 20 answers." },
  "context": { "issue_id": "ISS-1042" }
}

The same holds for the other agents: correlate_changes completes with the matching changes as an artifact; publish_guidance completes when guidance is live — immediately for rep-facing guidance, after CX Lead approval for customer-facing.

Tradeoffs

What I'd change for production

  • Version the data model and every interface. Issue and Case carry a schema version, and the Core API, webhook events, and MCP tools are versioned separately. Enterprises can then move up a tier, or stay put, without anything breaking underneath them.
  • Offline evals for classification and clustering. Build a labeled set of conversations, and measure each signal's agreement with human labels plus cluster precision and recall. No model or prompt change ships unless it holds or improves those numbers.

What I'd measure

  • The metrics in the Metrics tab: the north star and each stage's primary metric, each with its guardrail.
  • Tier adoption and migration friction: how many enterprises are on each tier, and what it costs to move up (time to onboard, integration issues along the way). If moving up is hard, "no re-integration" isn't true yet.
  • MCP usage: which tools engineers call, how often, and whether a session ends in an action (ask_customers, mark_resolved). Tools nobody calls mean I guessed the wrong intents.

What I'm unsure about

  • Webhooks. I don't know yet whether product teams want issues pushed into their tracker, or a different channel such as Slack, on-call paging, or a digest. I'd validate with a handful of product teams before building more than one channel.
  • A2A. I don't know whether enterprises will run their own release, engineering, and KB agents, or whether A2A is the protocol they'll adopt. I'd build it with one or two design partners before generalizing.
